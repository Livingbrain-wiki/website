FAQ

Questions.

Living Brain is open in early access at app.livingbrain.wiki. Answers marked planned describe what is coming.

Product

What does "living" mean?

Most wikis start going stale the day they're written. Living Brain keeps dated facts: when something changes, the old fact keeps the dates it held and the new one becomes current. A background pass called dreaming tidies each subject's facts: it ends, supersedes, retracts or renames outdated ones and keeps the history. Merging duplicate pages, flagging contradictions, rewriting summaries on a schedule, and the learning layer that learns how each person works, are planned.

How is it different from Slack AI or Glean?

Search tools find what already exists. Living Brain writes and maintains a wiki you own as Markdown, cites the message behind every fact, acts in your tools, gives your coding harnesses the same memory, and is priced per workspace instead of per seat.

Which chat apps does it work with?

Slack and Discord, as equals, are planned. WhatsApp and Telegram come later. You don't need a chat app: the web app and the Mac app work on their own, the livingbrain command-line tool ships inside the Mac app, and any coding harness that supports MCP can connect to the live MCP endpoint.

Is there an app?

Yes, in early access: sign in with your email at app.livingbrain.wiki. Living Brain for Mac, a menu bar app, syncs Claude Code and Codex sessions from the repositories you choose and opens your wiki in a window. Planned: a web app that installs from your browser on phone and desktop as a PWA, keeps pages readable offline, opens on one box to search or ask, and keeps the 3D brain one tap away.

Can I have more than one workspace?

Yes. While signed in, choose New workspace from the workspace menu, or go to Settings, then Account. Each workspace has its own pages, sources and members. The Mac app keeps one sign-in per workspace and switches between them.

Can we put our own brand on it, or build on it?

Planned. On Teams and Crew, the app runs on your own domain with your name, logo and colours, and installs on phones and desktops as your app. A small "powered by" link is optional. There's also an open API with webhooks and SDKs for TypeScript, Python and Rust, so you can build the brain into your own tools. If you self-host, the code is yours to rebrand.

Coding harnesses

Which coding harnesses can I connect?

Any harness that supports MCP can connect to the live MCP endpoint, https://mcp.livingbrain.wiki/v1/pages/mcp, with a token. The harnesses we test are listed in waves, and each stays planned until its integration test passes: first Claude Code, Codex, Hermes, Cursor, OpenCode and Claude Desktop, then Gemini CLI, GitHub Copilot, Goose, Cline, Aider, Kimi CLI, Qwen Code and Pi. We're not affiliated with these tools; they connect through the open protocol.

Do you store my agent prompts and code?

Only if you opt in. Prompts and session notes are stored with secrets redacted first. Source code is never stored unless you allow it.

Can it collect my coding harnesses' logs?

Yes, for Claude Code and Codex. Living Brain for Mac and the livingbrain CLI sync those sessions from the repositories you opt in, and redact secrets and email addresses on your machine before anything is sent. Sessions open as full transcripts with tool calls, and subagent sessions nest under the call that started them. Cursor, OpenCode and Colonizer sessions, and token and cost breakdowns per person, repo and model, are planned.

Integrations

Can it connect my Gmail or Outlook?

Planned. The simplest and safest way needs no mailbox access at all: forward, BCC or auto-forward mail to your brain's own address on Owlpost, our sister email service, and only what you send arrives. If you want a whole mailbox kept in sync, you can also connect Gmail, Outlook.com or Microsoft 365, or any IMAP mailbox, plus your calendar, read-only. Your mail stays in your personal memory, encrypted with your own key, and no one else can draw on it unless you share a specific label or folder. Every message is screened for spoofing and prompt injection before the brain reads it, and mail can never trigger actions. Revoke the connection at any time and its stored mail is erased. When you self-host, you use your own Google or Microsoft app, so mail never touches our servers.

Can I import my ChatGPT history?

Yes. Export your data from ChatGPT (Settings, Data controls, Export data), then run livingbrain import chatgpt with the zip. It reads the zip on your machine, shows a preview, redacts secrets, and uploads only after you confirm. Claude exports work the same way. Markdown and text folders can be imported from the CLI or the Imports page. The step-by-step guide is at https://livingbrain.wiki/guides/import-chatgpt/

Does it work with Grafana and our logs?

Planned. Connect Grafana with a read-only token and the brain can answer "is prod down?" with the current numbers and a link to the exact panel. It reads your logs too, from Loki, Elasticsearch, Datadog, CloudWatch, Cloudflare or Sentry, and says what changed: when the errors started, which deploy came before, and the most common messages, with secrets redacted. Alerts become incident pages linked to the deploy and pull request that caused them, and the nightly pass drafts the postmortem for a person to confirm. In the other direction, Living Brain exports its own metrics, traces and logs over OpenTelemetry, so you can watch it in Grafana, Datadog or Honeycomb.

Can it answer my customers?

Planned, through SupportGenius, a sister Factory Zero venture for customer support. It answers customers only from pages your team has explicitly published to customers, with citations, and never from internal, private-channel or personal memory. When it can't answer, it asks the brain who owns the topic and routes the question to that person. Customer messages are screened for hidden instructions before they touch the brain.

Security

Is my data encrypted?

Yes, for pages, sources and temporal facts. Each scope (the shared brain, each person, each private scope) has its own data key, wrapped by a key held by the service and stored as a Worker secret. Page bodies and fact values are sealed with XChaCha20-Poly1305, and search uses a blind index, so a query touches only the scopes you can see. The server holds the wrapping key and decrypts to serve and answer, so this is encryption at rest, not end-to-end: the service can read your data. Slugs, subjects and attribute names are stored in plain text. Forgetting a scope deletes its rows and destroys its key. Bring your own key on Teams, and self-hosting for full control, are planned.

Is my data used for training?

No. Your workspace's messages and pages are never used to train models, ours or anyone else's. If you bring your own LLM, requests go to your provider under your account's terms.

Can someone hide instructions in a message to trick my agents?

Planned. Every source is screened for hidden text by PromptDecode, a sister Factory Zero venture: invisible Unicode tag characters, bidi controls and variation selectors are decoded and flagged before a page is written, and again before context is served to an agent. Anything that decodes to an instruction is held for a person to review. Mail also passes Owlpost's spoofing and prompt-injection checks.

Pricing

Can I self-host?

Yes. The Community plan is free for up to 5 people: it runs on your own Cloudflare account with your own LLM key. Larger teams need a Teams license, planned at $5 a month per workspace with unlimited people. The plan and the code as it lands are public on GitHub, open core: Apache-2.0, with the Teams features under a commercial license.

Which models?

On every plan you choose your own models, per role (triage, answers, research): Anthropic, OpenAI, OpenRouter, your LiteLLM gateway, or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint, including self-hosted ones. Crew also includes $5 of DeepSeek credit each month, so you can start without a key of your own; past that, add your own key or top up.

What does a question cost?

Planned, for hosted plans: writing the brain (reading sources and writing pages, with the nightly evolve pass included) costs $1 per million tokens, and reading it (search, pages and context for agents) is unlimited. Harder questions are priced per question by reasoning level: Minimal $0.001, Low $0.005 (the default), Medium $0.02, High $0.05, Max $0.25. Crew's $5 monthly DeepSeek credit covers about 1,000 questions at the default level. On your own LLM key you pay only your provider, with no fee from us. You can also run brain jobs on your own Claude Code or Codex subscription from the Mac app; Living Brain never holds those credentials.

How much storage is included?

Hosted Teams includes 10 GB and Crew includes 25 GB, covering wiki pages, sources, search indexes and aggregated agent logs. Extra hosted storage is $0.25 per GB a month. Self-hosted plans use your own Cloudflare storage, with no limit from us. Planned pricing.

Who will bill me?

Paid plans are sold by Polar, our Merchant of Record, which handles VAT and sales tax. Nothing is on sale yet.
